TL;DR

CB was built end-to-end in Qiskit and validated on 1, 2, and 3 qubits across seven experiments. The pipeline reproduces the analytic per-Pauli fidelities λ_P within shot noise for every channel — and, the headline result, catches a real two-qubit crosstalk error on a real entangling gate (CZ): under correlated ZZ noise, the measured λ_P for {XX, XY, YX, YY} jumps from the independent-noise baseline 0.81 to 1.00 (Δ = +0.19), while the other 11 Paulis stay flat. That four-Pauli cluster is the signature that SRB averages away.

1 · Methods

Slides cover the CB protocol (Meeting 3, slides 39–48). Recap of the bits this report uses, in slide notation:

Object Form What it is
Ideal map / noisy map G : ρ ↦ GρG† / Ĝ = Λ∘G ideal vs hardware
Ideal circuit C(P) = G^m(P) = εP classical answer key (since G^m=I)
Noisy circuit Ĉ(ρ) = R_m∘Ĝ∘…∘R_1∘Ĝ∘R_0(ρ) run on Aer
Single-run overlap f_{P,m,l} = Tr[C(P)·Ĉ(ρ)] =+1 if noiseless
Sequence average F_{P,m} = (1/L)·Σ_l f_{P,m,l} over L random Pauli draws
Twirled noise (per direction) Λ_T(P) = λ_P · P the diagonal Pauli channel CB measures
Two-length ratio (SPAM-free) λ_P = (F_{P,m₂}/F_{P,m₁})^{1/(m₂−m₁)} α cancels
Process fidelity F_RC = (1/K)·Σ_P λ_P one number from K Paulis
Error per cycle r_RC = 1 − F_RC headline error

Knobs

Knob Setting Why
G I, H, CZ (disjoint-pair layer) I = flat baseline; H, CZ = non-Pauli Cliffords, they rotate P
m₁, m₂ 4, 8 (N≥2); 4, 12 (N=1) both multiples of cycle order ⇒ G^m = I
K all 4ᴺ−1 for N≤2; random 30 of 63 for N=3 full coverage where it fits
L 30 per (P, m) shot-noise vs runtime
shots 100 (N=1), 200 (N≥2) shot floor matters as λ_P → 0
sim backend Aer; noise on a labelled UnitaryGate("CB_Cycle") clean noise injection (IRB trick)

How CB resolves one number per Pauli direction

The Pauli twirl over the random R_i forces the noise on Ĝ to a diagonal Pauli channel: Λ_T(P) = λ_P · P — each direction decays independently with its own scalar λ_P (slide 46–47). One dressed cycle pulls one factor of λ_P for the running observable; m cycles stack to λ_P^m, giving F_{P,m} = α · λ_P^m (slide 48). The two-length ratio cancels α and leaves λ_P per direction.

How we predict λ_P (for sim validation)

For a Pauli channel {p_Q}, the commute formula gives the bare per-direction decay:

λ_P^bare = Σ_Q p_Q · (+1 if [Q,P]=0 else −1)

Each error Q that anticommutes with P flips the observable's sign, contributing −1. When G rotates P between cycles (H, CZ), the recovered λ_P is the geometric mean over P's orbit of the bare λ's — derived inline at Exp 5.

Two detectors, two jobs

Detector Needs the noise? Where it lives
orbit-geomean predictor yes (probs, or marginal_probs for correlated noise) simulator — inject-and-recover validation
factorization_test on measured λ_P no — pure self-consistency hardware check; off in sim (gated by CBConfig.factorization=False)

In sim the orbit predictor is the honest yardstick. Factorization will become the hardware self-consistency check once an orbit-aware fix lands for rotating gates (open for #6).

2 · Experiments

Each section: setup → C(P) and Ĉ(ρ) → how noise determines λ_P → results → walk one critical Pauli → verdict. Numbers are pulled verbatim from the notebook outputs.

Exp 1 — N=1, G=I, independent noise

Setup. N=1, K=3 (X, Y, Z), G=I, m₁=4, m₂=12, shots=100, L=30. Four noise channels: depolarizing(p=0.05) · dephasing{I:0.95, Z:0.05} · bit_flip{I:0.95, X:0.05} · biased{I:0.90, X:0.03, Y:0.02, Z:0.05}.

C(P) = I⁴(P) = P (G=I, ε=+1; same form at m₁=4 and m₂=12). Ĉ(ρ) at m₁=4 — Ĝ = Λ (since G=I); five 1-qubit random Paulis R_i ∈ {I, X, Y, Z}, four Λ insertions: Ĉ(ρ) = R_4 ∘ Λ ∘ R_3 ∘ Λ ∘ R_2 ∘ Λ ∘ R_1 ∘ Λ ∘ R_0(ρ). After the twirl each Λ acts as Λ_T on the running observable, pulling out one factor of λ_P per cycle → F_{P,4} = α · λ_P^4.

The split into individual Paulis happens before the math — at protocol design. A single pass of the protocol = one chosen P. Concretely:

  1. Pick one P up front (say P=X for this pass).
  2. Prepare the +1 eigenstate of that P as the input ρ. For P=X, ρ = |+⟩⟨+|; for P=Z, ρ = |0⟩⟨0|; etc.
  3. Run the dressed circuit Ĉ(ρ) (the chain R_4 ∘ Λ ∘ R_3 ∘ Λ ∘ ... written above).
  4. Measure ⟨P⟩ at the end — just the one P you started with. That gives a single number f_{P,m,l}.
  5. Average over L sequences → F_{P,m}. Run at m₁=4 and m₂=12 → ratio → λ_P for that one direction.

So F_{P,4} = α · λ_P^4 is per pass — it's F_{X,4} = α · λ_X^4 for the X pass, F_{Y,4} = α · λ_Y^4 for the Y pass, etc. They're literally three independent experiments. Three preps, three measurements, three two-length ratios → three rows in Exp 1's table. (Same K-passes-K-rows logic holds for Exp 2–7; K just grows.)

Noise → λ_P. Commute formula directly. Predicted triples (λ_X, λ_Y, λ_Z):

Noise predicted (λ_X, λ_Y, λ_Z)
depol(p) (1−p, 1−p, 1−p)
dephasing(q) (1−2q, 1−2q, 1)
bit_flip(q) (1, 1−2q, 1−2q)
biased (p_I+p_X−p_Y−p_Z, p_I+p_Y−p_X−p_Z, p_I+p_Z−p_X−p_Y)

dephasing 0.9000 → 0.8985 0.9000 → 0.9026 1.0000 → 1.0000 0.9333 → 0.9337

Exp 2 — N=1, G=H, independent noise

Setup. Same as Exp 1 but G=H (Hadamard, order 2). m₁=4, m₂=12 both even.

C(P) = H⁴(P) = P (H²=I ⇒ H⁴=I; ε=+1). Ĉ(ρ) at m₁=4 — Ĝ = Λ∘H; same five 1q random Paulis as Exp 1: Ĉ(ρ) = R_4 ∘ Λ∘H ∘ R_3 ∘ Λ∘H ∘ R_2 ∘ Λ∘H ∘ R_1 ∘ Λ∘H ∘ R_0(ρ). Between cycles the H factor rotates the running observable: X ↔︎ Z.

What's different. H is a non-Pauli Clifford. Between cycles, H rotates the observable: H X H = Z and H Z H = X (and H Y H = −Y). So a cycle on X looks like noise on X, then on Z, then on X, … alternating. The recovered λ_X mixes the bare λ_X and bare λ_Z.

Noise → λ_P. The bare commute formula still gives λ_X^bare, λ_Z^bare; the cycle's recovered λ_X is the geometric mean over the orbit {X, Z} — i.e. λ_X^recovered = √(λ_X^bare · λ_Z^bare). (Full derivation in Exp 5.)

Results.

Noise λ_X λ_Y λ_Z F_RC (exp → meas)
depolarizing 0.9500 → 0.9488 0.9500 → 0.9563 0.9500 → 0.9561 0.9500 → 0.9537
dephasing 0.9487 → 0.9483 0.9000 → 0.8861 0.9487 → 0.9473 0.9325 → 0.9272
bit_flip 0.9487 → 0.9463 0.9000 → 0.8965 0.9487 → 0.9501 0.9325 → 0.9310
biased 0.8798 → 0.8830 0.8400 → 0.8415 0.8798 → 0.8702 0.8665 → 0.8649

Walking the critical Pauli — dephasing on X. Bare: λ_X^bare = 0.90, λ_Z^bare = 1.00. Through H, X rotates to Z and back. Orbit {X, Z}. Predicted λ_X^recovered = √(0.90 · 1.00) = 0.9487. Measured 0.9483. The protected Z axis is shared with X across the orbit — there is no axis the H cycle leaves fully protected.

Verdict. Non-Pauli Clifford handled correctly. The "protected axis" behaviour we saw in Exp 1 partly collapses to the orbit geomean — exactly the machinery CZ will need at N=2.

Exp 3 — N=2, G=I, independent noise

Setup. N=2, K=15 (all non-identity 2q Paulis), G=I, m₁=4, m₂=8, shots=200, L=30. Same four noise channels, each tensored to 2 qubits (error fires independently on each qubit).

C(P) = I⁴(P) = P, ε=+1. Ĉ(ρ) at m₁=4 — same chain shape as Exp 1; R_i now 2-qubit Paulis (16 options each) and Λ is the tensored 2q channel: Ĉ(ρ) = R_4 ∘ Λ ∘ R_3 ∘ Λ ∘ R_2 ∘ Λ ∘ R_1 ∘ Λ ∘ R_0(ρ).

Noise → λ_P. Independent per-qubit noise ⇒ joint Pauli λ_{A⊗B} should factorize:

λ_{A⊗B} = λ_A · λ_B

This is the factorization predicted from the per-qubit commute formula.

Exp 4 — N=2, G=I, correlated ZZ noise

Setup. Same as Exp 3, but a new noise channel: a single 2-qubit Pauli error, {II:0.95, ZZ:0.05}. Both qubits get hit by Z together or not at all — it is not a tensor of two 1-qubit channels.

C(P) = P, ε=+1. Ĉ(ρ) at m₁=4 — same chain as Exp 3, but Λ is now the single 2-qubit Pauli error {II:0.95, ZZ:0.05} (not a tensor of two 1q channels): Ĉ(ρ) = R_4 ∘ Λ ∘ R_3 ∘ Λ ∘ R_2 ∘ Λ ∘ R_1 ∘ Λ ∘ R_0(ρ).

Noise → λ_P. Same commute formula, now over 2-qubit errors. The independent baseline comes from the marginal of this noise: each qubit alone sees {I:0.95, Z:0.05} — i.e. dephasing-0.05 per qubit. So Exp 3's dephasing prediction is what we compare against. The data on whether the noise is really independent or not is in the per-Pauli λ_P.

Results.

Pauli class predicted (indep. baseline) measured Δ
IX, XI, IY, YI (single-X/Y marginals) 0.9000 0.8890 – 0.9055 ≈ 0
IZ, ZI, ZZ (Z-immune) 1.0000 1.0000 0
XZ, ZX, YZ, ZY (one X/Y, one Z) 0.9000 0.8847 – 0.9065 ≈ 0
XX, XY, YX, YY 0.8100 1.0000 +0.1900 ⚡
F_RC 0.8960 0.9447 +0.0487

Walking the critical Pauli — XX. Under correlated {II:0.95, ZZ:0.05}:

  • The ZZ error fires (probability 0.05). It hits both qubits with a Z.
  • The XX observable measures X on each qubit. Z anticommutes with X, so each side flips → sign flips on both qubits in the same shot → (−1)(−1) = +1.
  • The XX reading is +1 even when the error fires. Verdict: λ_XX = 1.0 exactly.

The independent prediction would have been λ_X · λ_X = 0.90 · 0.90 = 0.81. The gap +0.19 is the crosstalk fingerprint: only correlated Zs on both qubits can cancel into +1 on XX. Independent dephasing cannot — two separate flips are not synchronised. Same logic applies to XY, YX, YY — both observables are "flippable" by Z, both flips fire together → cancel → λ = 1.0.

Verdict. CB has called out correlated noise. F_RC = 0.9447 > 0.8960 — crosstalk makes the average look ~5% better than the matching independent noise. The per-Pauli view reverses the verdict.

Exp 5 — N=2, G=CZ, bit_flip — and the orbit-geomean explainer

Setup. N=2, K=15, G=CZ (order 2), m₁=4, m₂=8, shots=200, L=30, noise = bit_flip{I:0.95, X:0.05} tensored.

C(P) = CZ⁴(P) = P (CZ²=I ⇒ CZ⁴=I; ε=+1). Ĉ(ρ) at m₁=4 — Ĝ = Λ∘CZ; R_i are 2q random Paulis; Λ is bit_flip tensored to 2 qubits: Ĉ(ρ) = R_4 ∘ Λ∘CZ ∘ R_3 ∘ Λ∘CZ ∘ R_2 ∘ Λ∘CZ ∘ R_1 ∘ Λ∘CZ ∘ R_0(ρ). Between cycles the CZ factor rotates the running observable around its orbit (see the rule below).

What's new. CZ is a non-Pauli Clifford — like H but on two qubits. It rotates Paulis between cycles, on a specific pattern:

CZ rule: X or Y on a qubit → drops a Z on the partner (and toggles I↔Z, X↔Y on it).
         I or Z on a qubit → frozen.

So CZ has fixed Paulis {ZI, IZ, ZZ} (every qubit holds an I or Z — nothing to rotate), and 2-cycle orbits for the rest: {XI ↔︎ XZ}, {IX ↔︎ ZX}, {YI ↔︎ YZ}, {IY ↔︎ ZY}, {XX ↔︎ YY}, {XY ↔︎ YX}.

The orbit-geomean rule (derivation)

A single dressed cycle is (R∘CZ∘Λ). After the Pauli twirl, the noise per position is Λ_T(P) = λ_P · P — but CZ rotates P → P' between cycles. So the observable that started as P is, at the next position, P' = CZ·P·CZ, which acquires Λ_T(P') = λ_{P'} · P'. After two cycles, P' → P again (orbit length 2). Over m cycles (m even):

⟨P, Ĉ(ρ)⟩  ∝  λ_P · λ_{P'} · λ_P · λ_{P'} · … (m factors)
            = (λ_P · λ_{P'})^{m/2}
            = ( √(λ_P · λ_{P'}) )^m

So the recovered decay is the geometric mean: λ_P^recovered = √(λ_P · λ_{P'}). The two-length ratio measures this — the geomean over the orbit.

Worked example — XI under bit_flip.

  1. Compute bare λ from the commute formula on the tensored channel {II:0.9025, IX:0.0475, XI:0.0475, XX:0.0025}:
    • λ_XI^bare: which 2q errors commute with XI? II✓, IX✓ (no overlap with q0=X), XI✓, XX✓. All commute → λ_XI^bare = 1.00.
    • λ_XZ^bare: II✓, IX anticomm (X on q1 anticomm Z on q1) → −, XI✓, XX anticomm → −. So λ_XZ^bare = (0.9025 + 0.0475) − (0.0475 + 0.0025) = 0.90.
  2. CZ orbit of XI is {XI, XZ}. Geomean: λ_XI^recovered = √(1.00 · 0.90) ≈ 0.9487.
  3. Measured: 0.9499. ✓

Results (all 15 Paulis).

Pauli expected measured Δ
IX, XI 0.9487 0.9428, 0.9499 small
IY, YI 0.8538 0.8593, 0.8492 small
IZ, ZI 0.9000 0.8992, 0.8870 small
XX, XY, YX, YY 0.9000 0.9069, 0.9093, 0.9011, 0.8980 all |Δ| ≤ 0.009
XZ, ZX 0.9487 0.9492, 0.9456 small
YZ, ZY 0.8538 0.8533, 0.8611 small
ZZ 0.8100 0.8133 +0.003

Verdict. The orbit-geomean predictor reproduces every measured λ_P within shot noise. CZ works.

Why this matters (and why factorization_test is wrong here). The factorization test asks: does λ_{A⊗B} = λ_A · λ_B hold? Under CZ, even with perfectly independent noise, no: e.g. λ_XI is now an orbit geomean (√(1·0.90) ≈ 0.9487), so λ_XI · λ_IX = 0.9487 · 0.9487 = 0.90 while λ_XX (also an orbit geomean of bare 0.90's) = 0.90. Numerically they happen to agree here but the framework is dirty — factorization_test will throw false alarms once the bare per-qubit λ's are anisotropic. The orbit predictor is the right yardstick on a rotating gate. (Fix for hardware is open under #6.)

Exp 6 — N=2, G=CZ, correlated ZZ — the payoff

Setup. Same as Exp 5, but noise = {II:0.95, ZZ:0.05} (Exp 4's correlated channel — now attached to a real entangling gate).

3 · Analysis

3.1 — Why exactly four Paulis light up

A Z error anticommutes with X and Y (sign flip) and commutes with I and Z (invisible). For a 2-qubit observable P = P_0 ⊗ P_1 under a ZZ error, the net sign is the product of per-qubit flips:

P_0 flippable? (X/Y) P_1 flippable? (X/Y) flips λ_P
both immune (Z/I) — 0 1.0 — {ZI, IZ, ZZ} (3 Paulis)
one flippable, one immune — 1 1 − 2q = 0.90 — 8 Paulis
both flippable — 2 → cancel 1.0 — {XX, XY, YX, YY} (4 Paulis)

The two flips cancel inside the same shot. Independent dephasing has the same per-qubit flip rate but the flips aren't synchronised — XX factorises to λ_X · λ_X = 0.81, not 1.0. That cancellation is the signature; the 0.19 gap is its strength.

3.2 — Why F_RC inflates instead of dropping

Crosstalk makes 4 of 15 Paulis look perfect, and F_RC = avg λ_P averages that up:

F_RC^indep      = (3·1.0 + 8·0.90 + 4·0.81) / 15 = 0.8960
F_RC^correlated = (3·1.0 + 8·0.90 + 4·1.00) / 15 = 0.9460
predicted gap   = +0.0500  ↔  measured (Exp 6) = +0.0474

Two qubits with correlated errors look ~5% more reliable on average than two qubits with the matching independent errors. SRB would report the higher number and call it a win. CB exposes the trade because it never collapses the 15 Paulis into one mean.

3.3 — Orbit predictor vs factorization_test on a rotating gate

factorization_test checks λ_{A⊗B} = λ_A · λ_B — exact under independent noise on the identity cycle (Exp 3 ✓). Under CZ this is no longer true even under perfectly independent noise: CZ rotates XI ↔︎ XZ, so the recovered λ_XI is a geomean over its orbit; λ_XX is a geomean over a different orbit; the product λ_XI · λ_IX doesn't equal λ_XX in general. The test would false-alarm.

The orbit-geomean predictor is the right baseline: follow P around its orbit (Q ← G·P·G† until it returns), take bare per-qubit-product λ at each step, geomean. Under independent noise this is exact (Exp 5 ✓). Under correlated noise the bare-product step is wrong on the "both flippable" Paulis → measurement pulls away → that gap is the honest crosstalk signal on a rotating gate (Exp 6 ✓).

3.4 — Scaling

Exp 7 is the first run that touches the three pieces that only appear at N≥3: the K-subset path (30 of 63 Paulis), 3-qubit orbits with a spectator qubit, and 3-qubit noise tensoring. All three behaved. No NaN at m₂=8 — the lowest expected λ_P was 0.729 (on ZZZ/YZZ), well above the shot floor. The data point needed before trying N=10 on a stabilizer simulator.
